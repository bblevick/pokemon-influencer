## Territory and weighting semantics

Native is an explicitly bound home territory. Nearby is a designated adjacent accessible area requiring the trainer's own recorded access/travel grant; connected-map chains and player visits grant nothing. Accessible is a farther explicitly bound map with progression/access plus a distinct actor-scoped acquisition permission. Exceptional requires explicit acquisition/story permission. Accessible and exceptional pools never become ordinary nearby pools.

Filter the pinned FireRed encounters by profile map/method, named stage and actor grants, species identity restrictions, level ceiling, finite capture equipment, spacing and party capacity **before** defining origin probabilities. Among nonempty eligible native/nearby pools, normalize the configured80/20 weights. With both pools eligible, probabilities are80/20; with only native eligible, native is1; with no ordinary eligible pools, there is no ordinary draw. Explicitly permitted accessible/exceptional alternatives are reported separately, never invented as an empty-pool fallback.

PREP's future seeded origin draw must select an origin category under these probabilities, then expose legal choices from that selected category to System One; System One may choose a species or keep the team unchanged. Store seed/draw/policy/scope evidence. Merely attaching preference labels to an unrestricted mixed pool does not enforce this rule. **No origin RNG sampler or System One selection was implemented here**, and no exact empirical80/20 final acquisition rate is claimed. Different pool availability, no-change decisions and catch outcomes mean even a correctly weighted process need not yield an80/20 completed-team ratio. Configurable profile weights support future70/30 or explicitly authored trainer exceptions without changing the resolver.

## Bounds, identity and versions

Available acquisition allowance is the minimum of the configured opportunity allowance minus prior opportunity acquisitions, explicit remaining campaign cap, remaining stage party capacity, native six-member capacity and finite equipment stock/cost. It never forces an acquisition. Unsupported mechanical methods remain unsupported; an inactive configured allowance is a policy restriction instead. No resource use, capture attempt, capture success, reserve roster or rotation is executed. A six-member party cannot keep catching merely because a numeric allowance is larger; later reserve/rotation behavior would require additional authorization and implementation.

Original companions remain required by default; signature lineages are always required. Evolution preserves the original individual lineage. Current companions are retained by this implementation; non-signature rotation has no issuer or active profile. Species allowlists restrict permissible new identities independently of geographic availability. Exact species/evolution/move legality remains the pinned catalog/ROSTER authority; these are partial policy prerequisites, fully_legal=false, not complete teams.

Move changes are a single per-opportunity budget across existing companions, including evolution learning, not a per-Pokémon budget. Profile values and opening defaults can be tuned (for example2→3) without rewriting the resolver. Candidate completeness/aggregate installation/accounting still belongs to PREP. No automatic learning, catch, item or identity exceptions arise to satisfy diversity.

Every shared resolution records contract/balance/profile hashes and scope. Version changes apply only to future uncommitted preparation opportunities. Already committed teams/history/resources retain their original policy hashes and decisions. Resolution does not inspect or write commitment records; actual version-adoption, one-time commit/lock and replay enforcement are downstream acceptance gates, not claimed live functionality. Current tests prove pure different-version outputs and unchanged inputs, not actual installed-team persistence.
